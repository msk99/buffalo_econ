00Scope and status

This document describes how the engine works. It is a method reference, not a results report. Results for the current parameter set, and the findings arising from them, are in the validation record; every assumption taken during the build is in the decision record; and the evidence behind each default value is in the parameter rationale document.

The model produces bank-ready financial projections — NPV, IRR, BCR, DSCR, payback, pre- and post-tax — for dairy buffalo (Murrah) enterprises of 5 to 1,000 adult buffaloes in North-West India, over 5- or 10-year horizons. It is delivered as an interactive web application and a downloadable Excel project report, both driven entirely by a single parameter workbook that domain experts edit directly.

Status

The engine is built and tested end to end, and the application is deployed. The validation gate remains open: the model has not yet been reconciled against two real reference project reports (a NABARD model report and a CIRB farm dataset). Until that reconciliation is signed off, outputs should be treated as methodologically sound but not yet bank-ready.

01Model framework

The engine is a bioeconomic model in the strict sense: three coupled sub-models — biological, technological and economic — fed by one data layer and drained by one delivery layer. Each sub-model answers a different question, and the coupling between them is one-directional, so no module reaches backwards and every number in the output can be traced to its origin.

Biological model
What does the herd do? Reproduction, lactation, parity, growth of young stock, mortality and culling — a monthly cohort projection that converts a target herd size into animal-months by class and litres of milk per month.
Technological model
What does production require? The production technology that a herd of a given size needs: feed quantities from a ration build-up, labour in fractional full-time equivalents, housing area, civil works, and machines from an equipment capacity model — physical quantities, chosen continuously in herd size.
Economic model
What is it worth? Prices the physical quantities, assembles the operating statement, applies Indian business taxation, structures the loan, and discounts one cash-flow timeline into NPV, IRR, BCR, payback and DSCR.

Why the layers are one-directional

Biology does not depend on prices, and technology does not depend on tax. Keeping the flow one-way means each layer can be verified on its own terms — conservation laws in the biological model, capacity arithmetic in the technological model, accounting identities in the economic model — and a change in any layer propagates forward predictably. It also makes the model auditable: a reviewer can reproduce any output line by walking the layers in order.

One timeline

The economic model builds a single cash-flow vector — all capital at t = 0, operations at the end of Years 1…n, terminal value inside Year n — and every metric reads that one vector. The justification is interpretability: NPV, IRR and BCR are only mutually consistent if they discount the same flows on the same clock. Computed this way, BCR > 1 exactly when NPV > 0 at the same rate, and IRR is the rate that zeroes the same NPV — properties asserted by automated test rather than assumed.

02The parameter workbook

Every number in the model lives in parameters_v3_2026-08.xlsx — 115 parameters across 14 sheets — because the people who know the right values are dairy scientists and economists, not programmers. The workbook is therefore designed as the primary interface: every parameter sheet uses the same eleven columns, so the loader reads any of them without special cases and a reviewer always knows where to look.

Three integrity rules

A spreadsheet is a forgiving medium, and a parser is not. Three editing rules close the gap between what a human writer might reasonably do and what a machine can safely read:

  1. No explanatory rows below a data block. A parser cannot distinguish a comment from a datum by looking at it; free text inside the data range is either silently discarded or silently mis-read, and both are unacceptable. Commentary belongs in source_note.
  2. No parameter name may appear twice anywhere in the workbook. With duplicate names, which value wins depends on physical row order — an invisible and fragile property of the file.
  3. No blank anchor cell. A blank is ambiguous between “zero” and “forgotten”, and the two have very different consequences. Enter 0 when zero is meant; the loader treats a blank as an error.

Validation

params_validate.R runs before anything downstream trusts the data, collects every problem, and reports them together so a reviewer fixes the workbook once rather than discovering faults one run at a time. Errors stop execution; warnings print and continue.

  • Names present, unique, lower-snake-case; modes and rounding values recognised.
  • Anchor cells populated and numeric wherever the mode requires a number.
  • Parameters declared constant genuinely equal at both anchors.
  • Every value inside its declared min–max range.
  • Parity shares sum to 1; lactation fits inside the calving interval; service period plus gestation reconciles with the calving interval (warning if it does not).
  • Ration component cost equals kg × rate, and stated totals equal the sum of components.
  • Equipment: positive capacities, sane thresholds, and a warning when two items activate at the same herd size — stacked steps produce discontinuities in results that no single purchase justifies (§3).
  • Every app default that names a source parameter resolves to one that exists.

03Scaling to a herd size

A tool used to support loan applications must produce results that are continuous and monotonic in herd size: two farmers whose plans differ by one buffalo should not receive materially different appraisals. That requirement drives the whole scaling design. There are no size categories anywhere in the model. Each parameter is defined at two anchor herd sizes — 10 and 200 adult buffaloes — and moves between them according to a scale_mode declared in the workbook, so the behaviour is stated by the domain expert rather than assumed by the code.

Logarithmic interpolation

Economies of scale are approximately power laws — unit costs fall with size at a diminishing rate — so interpolation runs on log n. The choice is empirical as well as theoretical: tested on shed construction rate, linear-in-n interpolation produces a 3.8× change of slope between segments, while linear-in-log-n produces 1.2× — very nearly a straight line, and closer to the underlying economics.

t = (ln n − ln n_lo) / (ln n_hi − ln n_lo), clamped to [0, 1] v(n) = v_lo + (v_hi − v_lo) · t at n = 50, anchors 10 and 200: t = ln(50/10) / ln(200/10) = 1.6094 / 2.9957 = 0.5372 milk price → 70 + (65 − 70) × 0.5372 = Rs 67.31 / litre purchase price → 130000 + (110000 − 130000) × 0.5372 = Rs 119,255

Clamping: never extrapolate

Above the upper anchor every interpolated parameter holds its large_200 value; below the lower anchor it holds small_10. A trend fitted to two points carries no information about what lies outside them, and holding flat is the conservative reading — it assumes no procurement, price or labour advantage beyond the last size for which there is expert evidence.

This does not mean cost stops rising above 200 head. Per-head values are still multiplied by herd size and the equipment capacity model keeps adding units, so a 1,000-head farm still costs about 4.9× a 200-head farm against a herd five times larger. What stops changing is the unit economics; the practical consequence is a change of slope at exactly 200 head, not a step.

Equipment: a capacity model

Machines are genuinely indivisible — a farm buys a whole chaff cutter or none — so equipment is the one place the model deliberately keeps steps. Each item carries a minimum herd size, an optional maximum, a capacity in head, a unit cost and a life:

units = 0 if n < min_herd or n > max_herd units = ceiling(n / capacity_head) otherwise

The design constraint is that activation thresholds are deliberately spread apart: 1, 18, 28, 35, 45, 50, 55, 65, 80, 95, 110, 121, 140, 160. Real capital lumpiness is decorrelated — a farm buys its first milking machine at one herd size, its first cooler at another, its first tractor at a third. Each individual step is small relative to the farm that takes it; dozens of steps landing on one herd size would manufacture a cliff that no single purchase justifies. No two items switch on together, and the validator warns if an edit ever makes them.

Tested across every herd size from 10 to 1,000: total capital is strictly increasing, capital per head falls smoothly, and the largest change caused by adding a single animal is 3.0% — the first tractor at 110 head, a real cost that should be visible.

Ration interpolation

Feed is not collapsed into a single cost. Each feedstuff row is interpolated on kg/day and rate/kg separately, then multiplied, so the ration stays visible and auditable at any herd size — a reviewer can ask “how much concentrate, at what price?” and the model can answer.

04The herd engine

herd_cohort.R — the biological model — advances the herd one month at a time over the full horizon (60 or 120 steps). The monthly step is what makes staggered purchase, a realistic Year-1 ramp, parity-dependent yield and heifer attrition representable at all — none of them can be expressed on an annual grid. A single 5-year run completes in about 0.3 seconds, so the finer step costs nothing perceptible.

Fractional animals

The projection is an expected value over a population, not a simulation of one particular farm. Animal counts are therefore carried as fractions throughout and rounded only for display; labour is carried as fractional full-time equivalents. The justification is numerical as much as statistical: integer rounding at small herd sizes manufactures artefacts — a 15% culling rate applied to 9 versus 10 head rounds from round(1.35) = 1 to round(1.5) = 2 culls, a 48% jump in a first-order revenue and replacement stream from one added animal. An expected-value model has no business containing such steps; whole numbers appear only where an asset is genuinely indivisible, which the capacity mode handles.

The fixed-herd policy

The adult herd is held at the target size and culling is the balancing variable. The justification is comparability: the tool appraises an enterprise of a stated size, and only a fixed herd makes a 50-head plan a 50-head plan throughout — a herd that drifts up or down is silently appraising a different farm each year. Every retained heifer that reaches first calving joins the herd; any resulting surplus is culled and sold at salvage. Voluntary culling is capped so that it never cuts so deep that a replacement purchase becomes necessary, and purchases only cover what the pipeline cannot.

available = A − deaths + maturities culls_base = min( A · cull_rate_m , eligible_parity_4plus , max(0, available − target) ) culls_extra = max( 0 , available − culls_base − target ) repl_buy = max( 0 , target − (available − culls_base − culls_extra) )

In the early years, before the home-bred pipeline matures at month 36, deaths are replaced by purchasing adults, so the herd is genuinely fixed rather than sagging by roughly 3% a year. From about Year 4 the pipeline takes over, purchases stop, and surplus culling begins. Voluntary culling draws from parity 4+ only, because that is what voluntary culling is — the removal of older, lower-producing stock.

Emergent behaviour — for review

Steady-state culling settles near 25% a year, above the workbook culling rate of 15–20%. With 80% heifer retention the pipeline simply produces more replacements than voluntary culling and mortality absorb, and the fixed-herd policy culls the surplus. heifer_retention_pct is the lever if less churn is wanted.

Sequence within one month

  1. Purchase batches. The Year-1 herd arrives in staggered_purchase_batches equal instalments, staggered_purchase_interval_months apart; each enters parity group 2–3, matching the purchase-price basis of a 2nd–3rd-lactation Murrah.
  2. Adult deaths at the monthly-equivalent mortality rate.
  3. Pipeline ageing. Calves and heifers advance one month under class-specific mortality; males and non-retained females leave at the calf gate; the oldest heifer cohort reaches first calving.
  4. Steady-state balance — the formulas above.
  5. Apply to cohorts: exits are taken proportionally, joiners and any purchases are added.
  6. Parity advance. Animals move up one parity per calving interval; culls are drawn from parity 5+ downward; first-calvers enter parity 1 and purchases parity 2–3.
  7. Milk for the month, from the in-milk count and the parity mix.
  8. Births, split by sex ratio into the two pipelines.

Lactation timing

Purchased animals arrive freshly calved, so each batch is in milk from arrival for lactation_days, dry until its calving interval completes, and on the steady-state fraction thereafter — which is what produces the Year-1 production ramp rather than full output from day one. Rates given per year are converted with 1 − (1 − annual)1/12 rather than divided by twelve, so twelve monthly applications compound to exactly the annual rate.

Consequence — for review

Both purchase batches dry off together around months 10–16, so Year 2 shows a genuine dip in milk, turnover and DSCR. This is real for a synchronised purchase; more batches smooth it, and a 12–18 month moratorium — a workbook parameter — is the standard bank structuring answer.

05Milk production

One reproductive clock governs everything. Lactation length, dry period, calving interval and service period all describe the same cycle; if they are entered independently they can disagree, and the herd's in-milk share — the single largest driver of revenue — becomes ill-defined. The model therefore takes calving interval as primary, derives dry days from it, and retains service period only as a validator cross-check.

CI_days = calving_interval_months × 30.42 dry_days = CI_days − lactation_days derived, validated > 0 f_milk = lactation_days / CI_days share of adults in milk at 50 head: CI = 15.46 months = 470.4 d → dry 170.4 d → f_milk = 0.6378

Parity back-solve

Yield is entered as the herd average across parities, which is what a domain expert can observe on a real farm. Peak-parity yield is back-solved so that the workbook's parity distribution reproduces exactly the entered average — the model can then vary yield by parity without the user having to supply a peak figure they would only be guessing at.

parity_weighted_mult = Σ ( share_i × yield_mult_i ) yield_peak = yield_herd_avg / parity_weighted_mult shares 0.275 / 0.375 / 0.225 / 0.125 (parity 1 / 2–3 / 4 / 5+) mults 0.80 / 1.00 / 1.00 / 0.90 → weighted 0.9325 at 50 head: 10.54 / 0.9325 = 11.30 L/day at peak parity

Monthly milk is then in_milk × yield_peak × parity_mult × 30.42, where parity_mult is the herd's actual parity mix that month — it starts at 1.0 for a purchased 2nd–3rd-lactation herd, drifts as home-bred first-calvers replace purchased animals, and settles back on the workbook distribution. Production therefore reflects the herd the farm actually has in each year, not a static average.

06Operating costs

Feed

Feed cost is the interpolated ration cost for each animal class multiplied by that class's population and the days in the month. Wastage is treated as already inside the ration rates, so it is not added a second time.

Labour

Workforce is sized on adult equivalents — a calf takes roughly a third of the care an adult does and a heifer roughly six-tenths, so they count at 0.3 and 0.6 — and carried as fractional FTE, consistent with the expected-value principle of §4.

fte_required = adult_equivalents / animals_per_worker fte_family = min( fte_required , family_labour_fte_cap ) if herd ≤ family_labour_max_herd fte_hired = fte_required − fte_family supervisors = herd_size / supervisor_capacity_head fractional FTE

Family labour is costed at the market wage and reported on its own line. Costing it makes a household farm and a hired-labour farm comparable — the work has an opportunity cost whoever does it — while the separate line lets the same amount be read as household self-employment income, which is how a family farm actually experiences it.

Supervision is a share of a manager's time, not a whole person hired at a threshold: a 30-head farm needs a fifth of a supervisor, not zero and then suddenly one. The workbook accordingly expresses supervision as a capacity (herd / supervisor_capacity_head FTE), which keeps supervision cost — some ₹3.6 lakh a year for a full supervisor — from landing on a single herd size as a step no real farm would experience.

Health and breeding

Breeding is costed per conception, because that is the causal unit: each calving requires one conception, and each conception requires a number of services. The cost blends artificial insemination (services-per-conception × rate) with natural service in the proportion the workbook states. Mastitis is modelled explicitly rather than buried in a flat veterinary charge — incidence drives both a treatment cost and a milk loss valued at the milk price — so the disease burden responds to the parameters CIRB actually measures.

breeding_per_conception = (1 − pct_natural) × ai_per_conception × cost_per_ai + pct_natural × natural_service_cost mastitis = adults × incidence/12 × ( treatment_cost + milk_loss_pct × annual_milk_value_per_animal ) insurance = adults × insurance_pct_animal_value × purchase_price / 12 vet = adults × vet_per_animal_year / 12

Other costs

Electricity, water, transport and miscellaneous are all stated per head at both anchors — a single measurement basis is a precondition for interpolation, since interpolating between a per-farm figure and a per-head figure is meaningless. Land rent applies at every herd size, including the smallest: land used for the dairy has an alternative use, and that opportunity cost belongs in the appraisal whether or not cash rent is actually paid.

07Capital and depreciation

Shed cost is area × rate — covered and open space per adult, each at its own rate — so the rate the user types is exactly the rate the model charges, and scale economies in construction enter through the interpolated rate rather than through any hidden transform. Sheds are sized on the adult herd, with young stock housed within the open-space allowance.

Working capital is working_capital_months (3, the usual appraisal norm) of the first full operating year's operating cost, financed within the project cost and recovered in the terminal year.

Two depreciation schedules

Book and tax depreciation are different systems serving different masters — the books show a reader the even consumption of an asset's life; the tax computation follows the written-down-value rules of the Income-tax Act — and the model maintains both, because using either alone misstates the other's output.

Relief for animals comes instead through s.36(1)(vi): a deduction when an animal used for the business dies, equal to cost less anything realised. The herd engine produces that stream directly, and tracks a purchased-animal pool separately so that home-bred animals — which carry no purchase cost — correctly generate no deduction.

Terminal value

Taken in Year n: buildings and equipment at straight-line book value, adults at salvage under the default terminal_animals_at = salvage regime (a market mode values them at the scaled purchase price instead), heifer inventory at heifer sale price, plus recovery of working capital. The default is deliberately conservative: a bank appraisal should not depend on an optimistic residual valuation of the herd.

08Taxation

Dairy income is not agricultural income. Agricultural income is exempt under s.10(1) and defined in s.2(1A), but dairying involves no basic operations on land — the test established in CIT v. Raja Benoy Kumar Sahas Roy. A standalone commercial dairy is taxed as business income, and a model that omits tax overstates both returns and debt-service coverage; the engine therefore models it in full and reports pre-tax and post-tax results side by side.

Every rate, threshold and slab lives in workbook sheet 11_Tax. Nothing tax-rate-shaped is written in code — rates change with each Finance Act and GST Council meeting, and a rate change must be a workbook edit, not a release.

Section 44AD presumptive scheme

For most farms in the modelled range the presumptive scheme is the binding computation. The model computes tax both ways each year and takes the lower, but only where the scheme is actually available — and it checks the turnover cap every year rather than trusting the toggle, because eligibility is a property of the year's turnover, not of the user's intention.

eligible = toggle_on AND entity ∈ { proprietor , partnership } AND turnover ≤ presumptive_44ad_turnover_limit Rs 3 crore deemed_profit = turnover × presumptive_rate_digital 6%, receipts assumed digital tax = min( tax_on_deemed , tax_on_regular ) when eligible

Losses under the regular computation are carried forward and set off against later years. A 50-head farm turns over roughly ₹1.1 crore and sits comfortably inside the cap; a 200-head farm crosses it in every steady year and moves to regular taxation.

Consequence — for review

Post-tax NPV per head therefore kinks downward above roughly 150 head, where turnover crosses the ₹3 crore cap. Pre-tax NPV per head stays monotonic. This is tax law rather than a model artefact, and is worth a sentence in any report shown to a bank.

GST

Fresh and pasteurised milk are exempt supplies, so a farm selling raw milk is not required to register at any level of turnover under s.23(1)(a) CGST — and consequently no input tax credit is available. GST paid on inputs is a real cost. It is therefore treated as a costing assumption rather than a modelled line item: all input prices in the workbook are GST-inclusive and no separate GST line is added, which matches how market quotations are actually obtained. A gst_registered regime switch is reserved for a future value-addition scenario, where registration becomes mandatory and input tax credit turns on.

09Finance and viability

Loan

The loan is loan_pct_of_project of the total project cost including working capital, less any capital subsidy. Repayment is equal annual principal on a reducing balance after the moratorium, with interest paid — not capitalised — during it.

subsidy = project_cost × capital_subsidy_pct loan = project_cost × loan_pct_of_project − subsidy margin = project_cost − loan − subsidy promoter equity interest_y = opening_balance × (loan_interest_rate − interest_subvention_pct) principal_y = loan / tenure zero during the moratorium

The three cash-flow vectors

t: 0 1 … n−1 n project_pre −project_cost EBITDA EBITDA + terminal project_post −project_cost EBITDA − tax EBITDA − tax + terminal equity −margin EBITDA − tax − interest − principal (+ terminal at n)

NPV, IRR, BCR and payback are computed from these vectors and nothing else. The project vectors measure the enterprise before financing — the question a lender asks — while the equity vector measures the promoter's own return after debt service, the question the promoter asks. Both are reported because leverage cuts both ways: it raises equity returns when the project out-earns the loan rate and depresses them when it does not, and a borrower deserves to see which side of that line the plan sits on.

Debt service coverage

DSCR_y = ( PAT + book_depreciation + interest ) / ( interest + principal )

Reported per year with its minimum and average, on profit after tax as banking convention requires. Years in which no debt is serviced return no ratio rather than an artificial infinity.

10Sensitivity and breakeven

A point estimate is not an appraisal. The tornado swings each driver ±10% and records post-tax project NPV, ranking the assumptions by how much they matter; the breakeven solvers invert the whole model on milk price using a root-finder, answering the two questions a bank actually asks — at what price does the project stop creating value, and at what price does it stop covering its debt.

The zero on interest rate is correct and worth reading carefully. Project NPV is measured before financing, so interest enters only through its effect on taxable income — and at 50 head the farm pays no tax, because s.44AD deems a profit that the s.87A rebate extinguishes. Interest therefore moves the equity return and DSCR, but not project NPV. At 200 head, where tax binds, the same driver becomes non-zero.

Two breakeven solvers are provided: the milk price at which post-tax NPV reaches zero (₹61.44/litre at 50 head against a workbook price of ₹67.31), and the milk price at which minimum DSCR reaches the bank norm of 1.25 (₹81.71/litre) — a materially more demanding test, and the more useful of the two for an appraisal conversation.

11Outputs

The Excel report writes ten sheets — summary, capital, herd, operating statement, financials, loan schedule, terminal value, cash flows, sensitivity, and any user overrides — to a caller-supplied path. The application passes a temporary file, because the hosting platform has no writable project directory.

The application is a thin interface over the engine. Its defining property is that every sidebar default is resolved from the workbook at the chosen herd size and updates when that size changes — the workbook remains the single source of truth all the way to the screen. A numeric input becomes an override only when the user moves it away from the workbook value, and every override is recorded and printed in the report, so a reviewer reading a downloaded appraisal can see exactly which assumptions were the expert defaults and which were the user's.

Performance

A single 5-year run takes about 0.3 s and a full tornado about 1.4 s, so the monthly time step is comfortably fast enough for interactive use.

No hard-codingApp defaults resolve differently at 50 and 200 head, and equal the scaled workbook value
The continuity test earned its place

During development it caught two live defects that no unit test would have found: a whole-person supervision step that put a ₹3.6 lakh cliff at 25 head (supervision is now fractional FTE, §6), and parameter overrides that were not re-flowing into derived quantities — which had silently made yield sensitivity near-zero. Property tests on the shape of the output guard against classes of error, not single mistakes.

13Assumptions register

Where a value or rule was missing or ambiguous, a defensible default was chosen and recorded rather than the build stopping. Each of the following is flagged for CIRB confirmation; all can be changed by editing the workbook rather than the code.

Known simplifications

  • No within-lactation yield curve (no Wood's curve) and no seasonality in fodder price or calving pattern.
  • Marginal relief on income-tax surcharge is not modelled; nor is the s.44AD(4) five-year lock-out on opting out, the old regime, Chapter VI-A deductions, or AMT.
  • No insurance payout is credited on animal death — conservative.
  • Equipment replacement cycles fall outside the 5- and 10-year horizons and are not modelled.
  • The NABARD back-ended subsidy-reserve convention is not implemented; capital subsidy, if any, reduces the loan at t = 0.
  • Loan repayment is equal-principal, not EMI.
  • Shed area is sized on the adult herd, with young stock assumed housed within the open-space allowance.
  • pregnancy_rate is retained as a cross-check only; conception cost is already carried by ai_per_conception.
Open — blocks bank-readiness

Two reference documents are still required: a NABARD or bank project-report template if a required format exists, and two real project reports for reconciliation. The model should reproduce both within an agreed tolerance before its outputs are relied on. Also outstanding: CIRB confirmation of the equipment thresholds and capacities, and a decision on whether the 72-day gap between the stated service period and the calving interval is real — it is currently a validator warning and would move the in-milk fraction materially.

BGlossary of financial and technical terms

Grouped into four categories. Definitions describe the treatment in this engine.

B.1 Financial and appraisal terms

Net Present Value (NPV)
The difference between the present value of all cash inflows and outflows over the project life, discounted at a specified rate. NPV > 0 means the project creates wealth. Reported pre- and post-tax, at both workbook discount rates, from the single cash-flow vector of §9.
Internal Rate of Return (IRR)
The annualised compounded return that makes NPV exactly zero — the maximum interest rate the enterprise could afford to pay on its financing without losing money. Reported pre-tax, post-tax, and on promoter equity.
Equity (promoter) IRR
IRR of the promoter's own cash flows: margin money out at t = 0, and operating surplus after tax, interest and principal thereafter. Leverage raises it above project IRR when the project earns more than the loan rate, and drags it below when it does not — the model shows both directions.
Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR)
Present value of inflows over present value of outflows on the same timeline and rate as NPV, so BCR > 1 exactly when NPV > 0 — a property enforced by test. Banks and agencies use it to justify loans and subsidies.
Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)
The farm's ability to cover debt payments from operating income: (profit after tax + depreciation + interest) ÷ (interest + principal), per year, on profit after tax as banking convention requires. Banks typically require at least 1.25.
Payback period
Years for cumulative net cash flow to recover the initial investment, interpolated within the recovery year. Shorter means lower risk.
Discount rate
The rate converting future cash to present value, reflecting time value of money and risk. Workbook defaults 12% and 15%; both are reported.
Margin money
The promoter's own contribution to project cost (typically 25%); the remainder is bank loan.
Moratorium
An initial period in which principal repayment is suspended; interest is still paid. A workbook parameter, and the standard structuring answer to the Year-2 dip.
Depreciation
Systematic allocation of an asset's cost over its life; non-cash. Two schedules are maintained — straight-line for the books, and written-down-value for tax, with livestock excluded from the latter (§7).
EBITDA
Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — total revenue minus total operating cost, including replacement animal purchases. The line at which the operating statement (§6) hands over to the financial layer.
Net cash flow
Cash in minus cash out in a year; unlike net income it excludes non-cash items such as depreciation.
Working capital
Funds covering day-to-day operating expense — three months of recurring cost, the usual appraisal norm — financed within the project cost and recovered at the end of the horizon.
Terminal value / closing stock
Value of assets at the end of the business period: buildings and equipment at straight-line book value, adults at salvage under the default conservative regime (market optional), heifers at sale price, plus working-capital recovery.
Capital subsidy
A government grant (e.g. under NABARD dairy schemes) reducing project capital cost; historically 25–33% for eligible dairy enterprises. Default 0 in the workbook; the back-ended subsidy-reserve convention is a recorded future item.
Interest subvention
A scheme reducing the effective loan interest rate for eligible enterprises; a workbook parameter, default 0.

B.2 Taxation terms

Business income vs agricultural income
Agricultural income is exempt under s.10(1), Income-tax Act, but dairying involves no basic operations on land (CIT v. Raja Benoy Kumar Sahas Roy), so a standalone commercial dairy is fully taxable business income.
Section 44AD (presumptive taxation)
Deems business profit at 6% of digital turnover (8% cash) for eligible proprietors, HUFs and partnerships with turnover up to ₹3 crore, with no books or audit. The model checks the cap every year and applies the scheme only where beneficial.
Section 87A rebate
A rebate (₹60,000, FY 2026-27 new regime) that extinguishes tax for individual taxable income up to ₹12 lakh — the reason most small farms in the model pay no tax.
Written-Down Value (WDV)
The tax depreciation method: a fixed percentage of the declining block balance each year — 10% for buildings, 15% for plant and machinery — as against straight line in the books.
Section 36(1)(vi) animal write-off
Livestock cannot be depreciated for tax (s.43(3)); instead, when an animal used for the business dies or becomes permanently useless, its cost less any realisation is deducted. The model applies this to purchased animals only, since home-bred stock carries no purchase cost.
Surcharge and cess
Surcharge is an additional levy on tax above income thresholds (10% over ₹50 lakh, 15% over ₹1 crore); health-and-education cess adds 4% on tax plus surcharge. Both are workbook parameters.
GST exemption and input tax credit (ITC)
Fresh and pasteurised milk are exempt supplies, so a raw-milk farm need not register (s.23(1)(a) CGST) — and consequently cannot recover GST paid on inputs. All workbook input prices are therefore GST-inclusive and no separate GST line exists.

B.3 Biological and dairy terms

Murrah
A premium water-buffalo breed originating in Haryana, known for high milk yield (10–16 L/day) and high fat content (6.5–9%); the primary improver breed used globally for buffalo dairying.
Lactation period
The period of milk production after calving — 300 days in the current workbook.
Dry period
The rest between the end of one lactation and the next calving. Derived as calving interval minus lactation days, so the reproductive clocks cannot disagree (§5).
Calving interval
Time between successive calvings, 15–16 months for Murrah. The primary reproductive clock of the model: the in-milk fraction and calving rate both derive from it.
Age at first calving (AFC)
Age at which a heifer first calves — 36 months in the current workbook — and the length of the rearing pipeline.
Heifer
A young female that has not yet calved, reared from the calf gate to first calving on its own ration with its own mortality rate.
Parity
The count of calvings an animal has had. Yield varies by parity group (1, 2–3, 4, 5+); the herd's parity mix is tracked monthly and drives the average yield multiplier.
Culling
Removal of animals from the herd, sold at salvage value. Under the fixed-herd policy, culling is the balancing variable that holds the adult herd at its target size; voluntary culling draws from parity 4+ only.
Artificial insemination (AI)
Breeding with semen from genetically superior bulls. Costed per conception — services-per-conception × rate per service, blended with natural service in the workbook proportion.
Staggered purchase
Buying the Year-1 herd in batches at an interval so milk production ramps rather than starting all at once; batch count and interval are workbook parameters.
Heifer pipeline
The cohort-tracking of female calves through rearing to first calving, by age in months, with class-specific mortality.
Adult equivalent (AE)
Workforce-sizing measure in which a calf counts as 0.3 and a heifer as 0.6 of an adult, reflecting the relative care each class needs.
In-milk fraction
The share of adults lactating at a point in time — lactation days over calving interval days at steady state (≈0.64 on current values), computed per cohort during the Year-1 ramp.

B.4 Model and software terms

Bioeconomic model
A model integrating biological production processes — herd dynamics, milk production, reproduction — with economic analysis of costs, revenue and profitability, here through the three-layer framework of §1.
Decision support system (DSS)
A computer-based application that analyses data and presents it to help users make business decisions; here, the Shiny application over the engine.
Anchor
A herd size at which every parameter's value is stated by the domain expert — 10 and 200 head in the current workbook, read from the column headers. Values between anchors are interpolated; outside them, clamped.
Scale mode
The workbook column declaring how a parameter moves between its anchors: constant, log, linear, derived, capacity or regime (§3).
Capacity model
The equipment treatment: each item activates at a minimum herd size and is bought in whole units of a stated capacity, with thresholds deliberately spread so no two items step together.
Economies of scale
The cost advantage of size, expressed as logarithmic interpolation of unit costs and prices between the two anchors — approximately a power law, the empirically observed shape of scale relationships.
data.table
High-performance R package used for all tabular storage and computation in the engine.
Shiny / bslib
The R web-application framework (and its Bootstrap 5 theming layer) on which the dashboard is built; deployed at khatkar.shinyapps.io/buffalo_planner.
openxlsx2
R package used both to read the parameter workbook and to write the bankable Excel report.
NABARD
National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development — refinances agricultural and rural development lending, including dairy entrepreneurship schemes with capital subsidy; source of the appraisal norms used here.
ICAR-NDRI
Indian Council of Agricultural Research – National Dairy Research Institute, Karnal; premier dairy-science research institution in India and developer of the TEAM-CD appraisal software.
CIRB
Central Institute for Research on Buffaloes, Hisar — buffalo genetics, breeding and management research, and the source of the parameter values in this model.
